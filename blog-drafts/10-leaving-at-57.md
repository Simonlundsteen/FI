# After 35 years, walking away at 57: when "enough" means leaving a toxic job

> **In short:** A government employee with 35 years at the same job decided at 57 that they'd had enough of a toxic work environment. With a net worth of about $2.3 million, a $57,000 pension and a 401(k), and a financial planner's confirmation that the numbers worked, they retired, and described an enormous sense of relief.

## The starting point

Thirty-five years at one job. That kind of steady career is rare today, and it built a solid foundation: a pension, a 401(k), and a net worth of about $2.3 million.

## The turning point

The job had become toxic. As a government employee, they also felt pressure from political developments around their work. Turning 57 became the moment to ask: do I actually need to keep doing this?

## The plan

- **Get a professional second opinion.** A financial planner confirmed the numbers: they could retire.
- **Combine a pension with savings.** Their pension of about $57,000 a year isn't a fortune, as they put it, but it's something, and it's guaranteed for life.
- **Use the portfolio for the rest.** With about $2.3 million in net worth, the 401(k) and other savings cover the gap.

## The hard parts

Leaving after 35 years isn't just a financial decision. It's a goodbye to colleagues, routines and an identity. Coverage of similar posts in the FIRE community describes guilt about leaving teams and clients behind. And staying in a toxic environment for too long has its own cost, to health and happiness, that never shows up on a balance sheet.

## Life after FI

Their overwhelming feeling was **relief**: relief at no longer having to worry about work, and at no longer feeling they had to keep writing to their Senators and Representatives about developments affecting their job. Sometimes the best thing money buys is the ability to walk away.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Your money is a walk-away fund.** The point of saving isn't just retirement. It's having the power to leave a bad situation.
- **A pension is worth more than it looks.** $57,000 a year for life would take about $1.4 million invested to replicate.
- **Get a second opinion.** A planner's confirmation can turn anxiety into a confident decision.

## How to do this in the US

**Retiring at 57 hits a sweet spot.** If you leave your job in or after the year you turn 55, the **Rule of 55** lets you withdraw from that employer's 401(k) without the 10% early-withdrawal penalty. Public safety employees can use it from 50. At 59½, all retirement accounts open up penalty-free.

**Bridge to Social Security.** You can claim from 62, but each year you wait up to 70 increases your monthly benefit, by about 8% a year after your full retirement age. Many retirees with a pension and savings use their portfolio in their late fifties and sixties so they can claim a bigger Social Security check later.

**Plan for healthcare until 65.** Some public employers offer retiree health coverage. If yours doesn't, the ACA marketplace bridges the gap until Medicare.
