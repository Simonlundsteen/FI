# Retired at 30 on two ordinary salaries: the Mr. Money Mustache story

> **In short:** Two software engineers saved most of what they earned for about ten years, retired in 2005 just before he turned 31, and went on to start one of the best-known FIRE blogs in the world. The big idea: how soon you can stop working depends almost entirely on your savings rate, not your income.

## The starting point

Pete Adeney, the man behind the pen name *Mr. Money Mustache*, was a software engineer, and so was his wife. They weren't tech millionaires and didn't inherit anything. They simply had two decent salaries, and they decided early on not to let their spending rise every time their pay did.

## The turning point

Rather than one dramatic moment, it was a way of thinking. Adeney has said many times that the time it takes to reach freedom depends on one number: the share of your income you keep. Earn a lot and spend it all, and you'll work forever. Keep most of it, and you can be free in about a decade.

## The plan

- **Save most of every paycheck.** Reports put their savings rate at close to 70%, kept up for roughly ten years.
- **Avoid debt.** They retired debt-free.
- **Live well, cheaply.** Their spending stayed far below what their salaries allowed. Adeney is a keen carpenter, and doing things himself has always been part of the picture.
- **Invest the difference** in low-cost index funds and let it grow.

## The hard parts

The blog's no-excuses tone has drawn plenty of criticism. Supporters say he's being honest. Critics say it's easy to preach frugality from the position of a well-paid engineer. Both have a point, and it's worth remembering when you read his story: two engineering salaries are a big head start. The savings-rate lesson holds at any income, but the timeline won't be the same for everyone.

## Life after FI

"Retired" didn't mean doing nothing. Adeney stayed busy with carpentry, house projects, raising his son, writing, and later a small business on Main Street in Longmont. Retirement for him means never *having* to work for money again, not never working.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Your savings rate is your timeline.** Raising it from 10% to 50% cuts your working years by decades.
- **Don't let spending follow income.** Every raise you don't spend brings freedom closer.
- **Retirement is freedom of choice, not idleness.** Plan what you'll do, not just what you'll stop doing.

## What it would look like in Denmark

Their roughly $25,000 a year is about **160,000 kr.** With the 4% rule, that gives an FI number of about **4 million kr.**, or around **13,500 kr. a month** to live on.

A Danish household would need less for some things and more for others. Healthcare and schools are free here, which takes out a big American worry. But housing in the bigger cities costs more, and gains on investments outside pension accounts are taxed as *aktieindkomst* (27%, then 42% above a yearly threshold), so you'll need a bit more invested to take home the same amount. The core lesson carries over unchanged: a high savings rate is the fastest route, whatever your salary.
