# $5.2 million on a government salary: the public servant who retired at 52

> **In short:** He started working for the US government at 23 and retired at 52 with a net worth of about $5.2 million, a $90,000-a-year pension and subsidized health insurance. No stock options, no startup exit. Just maxing out his retirement plan from day one, buying a modest house, and 15 years of overseas postings with hazard pay and free housing.

## The starting point

He took a government job at 23. Government work isn't known for making people rich, and his story got attention precisely because of that.

## The turning point

There wasn't one dramatic moment. The key decision came at the very start: he began putting the maximum allowed into his government retirement plan right away, and never stopped.

## The plan

- **Max out the retirement plan from day one.** Starting at 23 gave his savings almost three decades to compound.
- **Buy a modest house.** He and his wife bought a small house and put only 20% down.
- **Take the hard postings.** For 15 years he worked in overseas locations that paid hazard pay and came with government-funded housing. Higher pay plus no housing costs is a powerful combination for savings.
- **Use every benefit.** He paid off his student loans through a government incentive program that encouraged employees to get more education.

## The hard parts

Fifteen years in overseas locations dangerous enough to come with hazard pay is a real sacrifice, for him and for his family. And as one follow-up article pointed out, the headline $5 million includes a $1.4 million home, which doesn't pay any bills. His investment portfolio is smaller than the headline suggests, though his pension more than makes up for it.

## Life after FI

He retired at 52 with three pillars under him: a large investment portfolio, a $90,000-a-year pension for life, and subsidized health insurance, which removes the biggest headache most early retirees face.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Starting early beats earning big.** Maxing out a retirement plan from age 23 did most of the work.
- **A pension is worth a fortune.** $90,000 a year for life would take more than $2 million invested to replicate under the 4% rule.
- **Count your house separately.** A paid-off home is great security, but it isn't income. Base your plan on what you can actually invest.

## How to do this in the US

**Federal employees** save through the **Thrift Savings Plan (TSP)**, the government's version of a 401(k), with very low fees and an employer match under the FERS retirement system. Many state and local government jobs offer a 457(b) plan, which has a useful quirk: withdrawals after you leave the job aren't subject to the 10% early-withdrawal penalty, whatever your age.

**Pensions change the math.** If you'll get a pension, subtract it from your yearly spending before working out your FI number. A pension that covers half your costs halves the portfolio you need.

**Retiree health insurance is gold.** For people retiring before Medicare at 65, employer-subsidized retiree coverage can be worth tens of thousands of dollars a year. Without it, the ACA marketplace is the usual route.
