# Ten years and a day: the couple who retired to travel the world and pay (almost) no income tax

> **In short:** Jeremy, from the US, and Winnie, from Taiwan, retired in their thirties to travel the world full time. Their blog *Go Curry Cracker* is famous for one thing in particular: showing how they lived on close to $100,000 a year of investment income while paying almost no income tax, completely legally.

## The starting point

Jeremy grew up in the United States. Winnie grew up in Taiwan and is the blog's photographer. Together they describe *Go Curry Cracker* as a love story about early retirement and world travel.

## The turning point

The goal was clear and specific: retire early and travel full time. Jeremy describes how long it took: *"From the day that early retirement and full time travel became a real goal to the day that I submitted my resignation, it was exactly 10 years and a day."*

## The plan

- **A fixed goal and a long runway.** Ten years of steady saving and investing towards one clear target.
- **Taxes as a major expense to plan, not an afterthought.** Their best-known posts show how they structured their income so that, for years, they paid nearly no income tax. One year, close to $100,000 of investment income came with a $0 income tax bill.
- **Travel as a lifestyle, not a holiday.** Living in different countries let them choose places where their money went far.

## The hard parts

Ten years is a long time to stay focused on a goal while friends spend freely around you. Their tax approach also depends on knowing the rules in detail and following them carefully, because tax laws change. What works one year can need adjusting the next.

## Life after FI

They've travelled the world for years and written in detail about the costs, the taxes and the everyday life of early retirees abroad. Their more recent base is San Miguel de Allende in Mexico.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Give your goal a date and stick with it.** "Ten years and a day" shows what steady focus can do.
- **Taxes are often your biggest bill in retirement.** Learning the rules where you live can save more than any coupon.
- **Where you live is a financial decision.** The same portfolio buys a very different life in different places.

## What it would look like in Denmark

The headline result, almost no tax on $100,000 of investment income, comes from the American system, which taxes long-term investment gains very lightly up to a fairly high income level. **Denmark doesn't work like that**, so don't expect to copy it.

Here, gains and dividends outside pension accounts are taxed as *aktieindkomst*: 27% up to a yearly threshold and 42% above it. But the lesson still applies, because the details matter a lot:

- A **couple has two thresholds**, so splitting investments between you can keep more of your gains at the 27% rate.
- An **aktiesparekonto** is taxed at only 17%, though deposits are capped.
- **Pension savings** have their own tax rules and timing, which affect how much you can spend when.

Spending time planning your taxes is one of the highest-paid hours you'll ever spend.
