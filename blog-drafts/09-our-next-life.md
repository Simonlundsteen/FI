# Burned out at the top: the political consultants who retired at 38 and 41 without pinching pennies

> **In short:** Tanja Hester and Mark Bunge worked long, always-on hours as political consultants in Los Angeles. They automated their savings, kept their biggest costs in check, moved to more affordable North Lake Tahoe, and retired at the end of 2017, at 38 and 41. Her book's subtitle sums up the approach: *Retire Early the Non-Penny-Pinching Way.*

## The starting point

Tanja and Mark worked as political consultants, a job with well over 40 hours a week and the expectation that they'd always be reachable. They earned well, but the pace was wearing them down, and their story is often told as part of a wider wave of burnout among professionals.

## The turning point

They realised that if they kept going the way they were, the job would take the best years of their lives. Instead of waiting for a traditional retirement, they decided to build their way out, on their own timeline.

## The plan

- **Automate it.** A large part of every paycheck went straight into savings, and another large part was automatically moved into investments before they could spend it.
- **Max out retirement accounts** and stick to a budget they set themselves.
- **Cut the big costs, not the small joys.** Tanja's main advice is to focus on housing and transportation, the biggest items in most budgets.
- **Buy less house than the bank offers.** When they bought in Tahoe, they chose a house well below what the lender would have approved.
- **Move somewhere cheaper.** Much of their savings was built while living in West Los Angeles, but they retired to North Lake Tahoe, which costs a lot less.

## The hard parts

Burnout is the real backdrop to this story. Always being on call takes a toll long before the money is ready. They also chose not to live an extreme life on the way. That made the journey more pleasant, but it took discipline to automate and protect their savings while earning well in an expensive city.

## Life after FI

They live in the mountains at Lake Tahoe. Their money comes from gradually selling index fund shares, rent from one rental property, and income from the *Our Next Life* blog and Tanja's writing. Tanja has written a lot about what early retirement is really like, beyond the numbers.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Automate your savings.** What you never see in your account, you never miss.
- **Focus on the big three:** housing, transport, and what the bank says you "can afford".
- **You don't have to be extreme.** A good income, sensible big decisions and automation can be enough.

## What it would look like in Denmark

The Danish version of "LA to Tahoe" is moving from Copenhagen or Aarhus to a smaller city or the countryside, where housing costs far less and the money you've built up goes much further. With more and more remote work, it's a realistic option for many.

Their automated approach is easy to copy here: set up an automatic monthly transfer to your *aktiesparekonto* or investment account on payday. Many Danes already have pension contributions taken from their salary before they see it. For early retirees, the trick is to also automate saving into freely available investments, so there's money to live on before the pension unlocks.
