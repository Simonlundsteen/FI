# Almost $5 million at 45, and still can't quit: the trap called "one more year"

> **In short:** A 45-year-old on r/fatFIRE has a net worth of around $4.4 million, not counting two homes. By any normal standard, that's enough to retire. But they can't bring themselves to quit. Instead they're weighing a lower-paid remote job, or staying three more years to add another million. It's one of the most common traps in FIRE.

## The starting point

Years of high income and steady investing brought this family to a net worth of about $4.4 million by 45, plus two homes on top. By most FIRE math, the finish line is well behind them.

## The turning point

The turning point hasn't happened yet, and that's the story. They want to quit. They're ready on paper. But every time they get close, the decision slips another year.

## The plan (so far)

They're weighing three options:

1. **Quit now** and live on their roughly $4.4 million.
2. **Switch to a remote job** that would cut family net income to about $350,000, with less job security than their current role.
3. **Stay about three more years** to add another $1 million to their net worth.

## The hard parts

**"One more year" syndrome** is the name for this, and it's extremely common among people who've saved for a long time. Saving becomes a habit and a source of safety, and the paycheck becomes part of your identity. There's always a reason why one more year would make things a bit safer. The danger is that "one more year" quietly turns into five or ten, and those are years you can't get back. As commentators on stories like this often say, being able to retire doesn't mean you have to. But if the job is making you miserable, the money may no longer be the real issue.

## Where they are now

Still deciding. Their story doesn't have a neat ending, which is exactly why it's worth reading for anyone who's getting close to their number.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Decide what "enough" is before you get there.** Write your number down, and agree with yourself (and your partner) that hitting it means something.
- **Time is the one thing you can't buy back.** Three more years for another million is a trade worth thinking hard about.
- **There's a middle path.** A lower-stress or part-time job can ease the transition. Some call it "Barista FIRE".

## How to do this in the US

**Test your number with a cautious withdrawal rate.** For a retirement that could last 45+ years, many planners use 3.25–3.5% instead of 4%. If your portfolio covers your spending at 3.5%, the math is very likely not what's holding you back.

**Think about healthcare before 65.** For many Americans, losing employer health insurance is the real fear behind "one more year". The ACA marketplace offers coverage to anyone regardless of health, and premium subsidies depend on your *taxable income*, not your net worth. An early retiree living off a taxable account can often keep their income low enough to qualify.

**Try a sabbatical first.** Many employers offer unpaid leave. A few months off can show you whether it's the job or work itself you want to leave.
