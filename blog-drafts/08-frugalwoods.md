# From city careers to a 66-acre homestead: how the Frugalwoods reached FI at 32

> **In short:** Liz and Nate Thames saved over 70%, sometimes 80%, of their take-home pay while working office jobs in Cambridge, Massachusetts. In 2016, when Liz was 32, they reached financial independence and moved to a 66-acre homestead in the woods of Vermont.

## The starting point

Things didn't start smoothly. After graduating from college, Liz struggled to find a job at all. She and Nate eventually built office careers in Cambridge, one of the most expensive areas in the US, but they increasingly felt that their busy city lives didn't match what they actually wanted.

## The turning point

They had a clear dream: a quieter, more meaningful life on a homestead in the woods. Once they saw that a very high savings rate could get them there in a few years instead of decades, they committed fully.

## The plan

- **Save almost everything.** In Liz's words, they *"ramped our savings rate up to over 70%, and sometimes 80%, of our take-home pay."*
- **Keep the city home as an asset.** When they left, they didn't sell their Cambridge home. They rented it out, so it now pays them a monthly income.
- **Buy the dream outright and modestly.** Their Vermont property, a house and 66 acres, cost $389,000.
- **Combine income sources.** Rent, blog income, and Nate's remote job for a nonprofit, which also provides the family's health insurance.

## The hard parts

Saving 70–80% of your income means saying no to almost everything most people consider normal: eating out, new clothes, holidays. Their FI is also built on several pillars, including Nate's job, which is partly about health insurance, a big cost for early retirees in the US. It shows that "retired" doesn't have to mean "no income at all".

## Life after FI

Liz left her career to write and run the blog, and the family lives on the homestead. Their life looks completely different from before, quieter and closer to nature, which was the whole point.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Know what you're saving for.** A vivid picture of the life you want makes saying no to everything else much easier.
- **Don't automatically sell when you move.** A rented-out home can become a steady income stream.
- **FI can be a mix.** Investments, rent and a job you enjoy can work together.

## What it would look like in Denmark

The homestead dream is very Danish in its own way. A house in the countryside, outside the big cities, costs a fraction of a Copenhagen apartment. Their $389,000 is about **2.5 million kr.**, and in many parts of rural Denmark that buys a house with land.

Two things make the Danish version different. Healthcare is free, so nobody needs to keep a job for insurance. And renting out a former home has its own tax rules here (rental income is taxed, and there are rules for owner-occupied homes), so do the math before deciding to keep your city apartment as an investment. The core idea carries over well: choose the life you want, then let your savings rate decide how fast you get there.
