# Bankrupt at 26, $1.5 million at 49: the single mom of three who did it with frugality

> **In short:** A single mother of three filed for bankruptcy at 26. More than two decades later, after relentless frugality and over 20 years with the same employer, she has $1.5 million saved, spends about $45,000 a year, and plans to retire at 55. Her own words: "behind everyone here, but still happy."

## The starting point

Her financial life started with a crash. She filed for bankruptcy at 26, the kind of setback that makes many people give up on ever getting ahead.

## The turning point

She had bought her first home in 2003. When home prices in her area shot up, she sold it for a significant profit and used that money as a springboard to rebuild her finances from the ground up.

## The plan

- **Max out every tax-advantaged account, every year:** her 401(k), her IRA and her health savings account (HSA).
- **Stay frugal for decades.** She shops at thrift stores, and she has taught her kids to do the same.
- **Keep cars for a long time.** Her car was bought used, and she's been driving it for 10 years.
- **Stay steady.** She's been with the same employer for more than 20 years.
- **Pay off the house.** She expects her mortgage to be paid off within five years, which will cut her expenses further.

## The hard parts

Raising three children alone while rebuilding from bankruptcy is about as hard as personal finance gets. There was no second income to lean on, and no big salary to make saving easy. She also felt she was "behind" compared to the stories she read in the FIRE community. But by the classic 4% rule, her $1.5 million already covers her $45,000 a year.

## Where she is now

She's still working, mortgage almost paid off, kids learning thrift-store savvy, and on track to retire at 55 if she chooses. Her title said it all: behind everyone, but still happy.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **A bad start isn't the end of the story.** Bankruptcy at 26 didn't stop her from becoming a millionaire.
- **Max out the tax-advantaged accounts.** Year after year, they did most of the heavy lifting.
- **Don't compare yourself to the internet.** "Behind" by Reddit standards still meant financially free by most others.

## How to do this in the US

**The HSA is the secret weapon in this story.** If you have a high-deductible health plan, a health savings account is triple tax-advantaged: contributions are tax-deductible, growth is tax-free, and withdrawals for medical costs are tax-free at any age. After 65, you can withdraw for any purpose and pay only ordinary income tax, just like a traditional IRA. Many FIRE savers invest their HSA and pay current medical bills out of pocket so it can grow for decades.

**Retiring at 55 has a special advantage.** Under the **Rule of 55**, if you leave your job in or after the year you turn 55, you can withdraw from *that employer's* 401(k) without the usual 10% early-withdrawal penalty (it doesn't apply to IRAs). Her timeline lines up with it neatly.
