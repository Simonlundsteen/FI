# $4.3 million, a new country, and a scarcity mindset: the part of FIRE nobody warns you about

> **In short:** A 41-year-old and their family left high-cost jobs, moved to another country and cut their spending from $150,000 a year to a planned $70,000, with a net worth of about $4.3 million behind them. Financially, they're more than safe. Emotionally, it's been harder: they describe a scarcity mindset and real uncertainty about "what's next".

## The starting point

They had what many would consider success: well-paid jobs in an expensive area and a lifestyle that cost around $150,000 a year. Over the years, they also built a net worth of about $4.3 million.

## The turning point

They decided to walk away from the high-cost jobs and the high-cost life, and they did it in a big way, moving their family to another country where living is much cheaper.

## The plan

- **Quit the high-cost jobs.**
- **Move abroad** to a place where their money goes much further.
- **Cut spending by more than half**, from about $150,000 a year to a planned $70,000.
- **Live off a portfolio that's far larger than they need.** At $70,000 a year, they're spending only about 1.6% of their net worth.

## The hard parts

This is the heart of the story. On paper, they're set for life several times over. Yet they described **a scarcity mindset**: after years of saving and protecting their nest egg, spending it, even modestly, feels wrong. They also wrote about **uncertainty about "what's next"**: when work no longer gives your days their shape, you have to build that shape yourself. Moving to a new country at the same time adds a whole extra layer of change.

## Life after FI

They're in their new country, with more money than they'll likely ever need and the hard work of building a new identity ahead of them. Their honesty about it is exactly what makes their story worth reading.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **The money is often the easy part.** Plan for the emotional side of leaving work as carefully as you plan your portfolio.
- **Give yourself permission to spend.** If you have a big margin, use some of it. A 1.6% withdrawal rate leaves a lot of room for enjoying life.
- **Don't change everything at once if you don't have to.** Quitting, moving countries and changing your lifestyle are three big changes. Spacing them out can make each one easier.

## How to do this in the US

**Moving abroad doesn't end your relationship with the IRS.** US citizens are taxed on worldwide income and must keep filing a federal return wherever they live. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion only covers *earned* income, not investment withdrawals, though long-term capital gains can still be taxed at 0% up to a certain income level. Some states also try to keep taxing former residents, so be careful about cutting state ties.

**Healthcare abroad** is often far cheaper than in the US. Many countries offer residents affordable private or public coverage. If you ever move back before 65, the ACA marketplace is your fallback.

**Social Security** is based on your 35 highest-earning years. Retiring at 41 means fewer earning years and a smaller benefit, but benefits can still be paid to you abroad in most countries.
