# Retired at 39 on $15,000 a year: the couple who proved you don't need $10 million

> **In short:** A married couple in Indiana retired at 39 with just over $1 million and now live on about $1,241 a month, around $15,000 a year. No kids, no mortgage, and no interest in keeping up with the neighbors. Their story is a direct answer to experts who say early retirement takes $10 million.

## The starting point

The couple posted their story in r/leanfire, the Reddit community for people retiring early on a small budget. What stood out wasn't a huge income or a lucky break. It was how little they needed to live well.

## The turning point

They decided early on what they *didn't* want: a big mortgage, an expensive area, and the pressure to spend like everyone around them. Once their costs were that low, the amount they needed invested to cover them forever shrank dramatically.

## The plan

- **Choose a cheap place to live.** They picked a low-cost area outside Indianapolis on purpose.
- **Own your home outright.** No mortgage means the biggest bill most families have simply isn't there.
- **Skip the status spending.** No keeping up with the neighbors, and no lifestyle that needs a big income to support it.
- **Keep taxable income low.** Their low income means one spouse qualifies for Medicaid, Indiana's health coverage for low-income adults, which looks at income rather than assets. That solves the problem of health insurance before Medicare, the biggest worry for most early retirees in the US.

## The hard parts

Not everyone cheered. Some Reddit users were skeptical that a millionaire couple should qualify for Medicaid. The husband maintained that their eligibility is fully legal, since the program looks at income, not assets. It's a reminder that early retirement plans built on government programs depend on rules that can change, and that they draw criticism.

## Life after FI

They live simply, debt-free and on their own schedule. With spending of about 1.5% of their portfolio a year, they have an enormous safety margin. Even a severe market crash would barely dent their plan.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Your spending sets your number.** $15,000 a year needs about $375,000 under the 4% rule. Their $1 million is far more than enough.
- **Housing is the big one.** A paid-off home in a low-cost area does more than a hundred small savings.
- **Know the healthcare rules where you live.** For US early retirees, health coverage is often the deciding factor.

## How to do this in the US

**Health insurance before 65** is the key piece of this story. In states that expanded Medicaid, eligibility for adults under 65 is based on income, not assets. If your income is above the Medicaid limit, the ACA marketplace offers premium tax credits based on your income, so early retirees who keep their taxable income moderate can often get affordable coverage.

**Keeping taxable income low** is easier than it sounds when you live on a portfolio. Only the gains on what you sell count as income, not the full amount you withdraw. Long-term capital gains can even be taxed at 0% up to a certain income level. And withdrawals from a Roth IRA (your contributions at any time, and earnings after 59½) don't count as taxable income at all.
