# FI at 39, retired at 43: the doctor who tested retirement before taking the leap

> **In short:** Leif Dahleen, an anesthesiologist in northern Minnesota, discovered the idea of financial independence in 2014, reached it at 39, and retired from medicine in August 2019 at 43. On the way, he aimed for a cautious 36 times his family's yearly spending and spent almost two years cutting back gradually.

## The starting point

Doctors often start their careers late and in debt, after many years of training. Leif was a practising anesthesiologist in northern Minnesota, a family man and outdoors enthusiast with a demanding but well-paid job.

## The turning point

In 2014 he came across the idea of financial independence and realised that with his income and a sensible plan, he didn't have to work until a traditional retirement age. Five years later, he retired.

## The plan

- **Set a cautious target.** His goal was savings and investments of **at least 36 times** the family's estimated yearly spending. That's a withdrawal rate of under 3%, well below the classic 4% rule, because a retirement starting in your forties may have to last 50 years.
- **Keep working past FI.** He reached FI at 39 but chose to keep working and saving for about four more years for extra safety.
- **Test it first.** For almost two years before leaving, he worked just one seven-day stretch a month, a trial run of life away from the hospital.

## The hard parts

Leaving a prestigious, high-paying career is hard. It's tied up with identity, status and years of training. In the US there's also health insurance: he faced a 22-year gap before government coverage starts, which simply has to be paid for out of pocket. Choosing a higher target (36×) and a gradual exit were his ways of handling that uncertainty.

## Life after FI

He has kept writing on *Physician on FIRE*, writing as a columnist for The White Coat Investor and appearing on podcasts about FIRE, especially for high earners. He describes himself as a former anesthesiologist, a family man and an outdoors enthusiast.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **A high income is a powerful tool,** but only if your spending doesn't rise to match it.
- **The earlier you retire, the bigger the safety margin you need.** 36× instead of 25× buys peace of mind for a long retirement.
- **Ease out instead of jumping.** A part-time trial run shows you what retirement feels like before it's permanent.

## What it would look like in Denmark

Danish doctors also earn well, but with higher taxes, so a bigger share of each pay rise goes to the state, and keeping spending low matters even more.

His 36× rule is worth copying for anyone planning a long early retirement. If your family spends 400,000 kr. a year, the classic 4% rule says you need 10 million kr. With 36× you'd aim for about **14.4 million kr.** That's a lot more, but it protects you against a bad start in the markets. A Dane does get one big advantage for free: there's no 22-year health insurance gap to fund, because healthcare is covered.
