# The Danish astrophysicist who retired in his early thirties on $7,000 a year

> **In short:** Jacob Lund Fisker, a Danish scientist working in the US, saved most of his postdoc pay, lived in an RV, and was financially independent within about five years. His blog and book, *Early Retirement Extreme*, made him one of the most radical and influential voices in the FIRE world.

## The starting point

Fisker was born in Denmark in 1975 and trained as an astrophysicist. He worked in research for about nine years, four of them in graduate school, which is not a field known for big salaries. What he did have was an unusual way of looking at money: as a tool for buying freedom, not things.

## The turning point

Working as a postdoc in the US, he realised how little he actually needed to be content. If his costs could be tiny, the amount he needed invested to cover them forever would be tiny too, and he could stop working long before everyone else.

## The plan

- **Spend radically little.** He and his wife lived in an RV in the East Bay, near San Francisco. In his own words, it allowed them to *"live in the East Bay Area spending less than $14,000/year for the two of us, including the RV."*
- **Save almost everything else.** As a postdoc, he's reported to have saved around 80% of his income.
- **Learn to do things yourself.** His philosophy is about building skills (cooking, repairing, making) so you need to buy less, not about doing without.
- **Invest the savings** so the returns cover his tiny costs.

## The hard parts

The word "extreme" is in the title for a reason, and Fisker has since said he regrets using the phrase. Most people find living on a few thousand dollars a year unthinkable, and it does take a very particular personality. He also didn't stop working completely: after reaching FI, he took a job as a quantitative analyst for a few years, until 2015, simply because it interested him.

## Life after FI

Fisker describes his life as semi-retired. He's funded partly by investment income and partly by occasional work he chooses. He's written about systems thinking, skills and resilience, and his online forum is still active, even though the blog itself no longer gets updates.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Your costs decide your FI number.** Halve what you spend and you halve what you need to save, and you save faster too.
- **Skills beat stuff.** Every skill you learn is something you never have to pay for again.
- **You don't have to go all the way.** Even a little of the ERE mindset can take years off your working life.

## What it would look like in Denmark

$7,000 a year is roughly **45,000 kr.**, about **3,800 kr. a month**. With the 4% rule, that's an FI number of only about **1.1 million kr.**

To be honest, living on that in Denmark today would be very hard, since rent alone usually costs more. But the principle is the point, and Denmark helps in some ways: free healthcare and education remove two of the biggest costs Americans plan for. Even a "Danish ERE" on 120,000 kr. a year would need only about **3 million kr.** invested. For a frugal couple saving most of two salaries, that's within reach in under ten years.
