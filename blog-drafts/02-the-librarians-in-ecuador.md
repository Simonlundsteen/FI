# Two librarians, no six-figure salaries, retired at 38 and 35

> **In short:** A couple who both worked as librarians, with peak household incomes of about $80,000 and $48,000, retired early, he at 38 and she at 35. They now live in Ecuador with their preschool-aged daughter on $2,500 to $3,000 a month. Their story answers a question many people ask: is FIRE only for tech workers with huge salaries?

## The starting point

Librarians don't make headlines for their salaries. At their peak, this couple's incomes were about $80,000 and $48,000. Solid, but nowhere near the six-figure tech salaries that dominate most FIRE stories.

## The turning point

Their story was shared in a discussion on r/leanfire about whether early retirement is only for people with big paychecks. The community's answer, backed by stories like theirs, was clear: your salary matters, but your spending matters more.

## The plan

- **Live well below a modest income.** On two librarian salaries, the gap between earning and spending is what built their freedom, not the size of the paychecks.
- **Invest the difference consistently** over the years.
- **Plan for early access to retirement money.** Their reported setup included a Roth IRA ladder, a way to reach retirement savings before 59½ without penalties (explained below).
- **Use geoarbitrage.** By moving to Ecuador, where living costs are much lower, they made their savings stretch much further than they would in most of the US.

## The hard parts

Moving abroad isn't for everyone. It means leaving family and friends, navigating a new language and culture, and figuring out healthcare and schooling in another country, all with a young child. And as one commenter in the same discussion pointed out, FIRE really is much easier on a bigger salary. This couple's path took discipline that a higher income would have made easier.

## Life after FI

They're raising their daughter in Ecuador, with both parents home and no jobs pulling them away. For many parents, that time with a young child is exactly the thing FIRE is meant to buy.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **You don't need a high salary.** A modest income with a big gap between earning and spending can still get you there.
- **Where you live is a financial decision.** The same portfolio buys a very different life in a lower-cost place, whether that's abroad or a cheaper US state.
- **FI can make the early years with your kids possible.**

## How to do this in the US

**Moving abroad doesn't free you from US taxes.** US citizens are taxed on their worldwide income wherever they live and must keep filing a federal return. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion applies only to *earned* income, like wages, not to investment income. But the low-income rules still work in your favor: long-term capital gains and qualified dividends can be taxed at 0% up to a certain income level.

**You don't have to leave the country** to get some of the same effect. Moving from a high-cost city to a lower-cost area of the US can cut housing costs dramatically.

**The Roth conversion ladder** is how many early retirees reach their 401(k) and traditional IRA money before 59½. Each year you convert a slice of your traditional savings to a Roth IRA, paying income tax on the conversion, which is often low when you have no salary. After five years, that converted amount can be withdrawn tax- and penalty-free. You need about five years of living costs from other sources to bridge the start.

**Low earners can save a lot through tax-advantaged accounts.** A 401(k) or 403(b) (common for library and public-sector jobs), plus a Roth IRA, can shelter a big share of a modest income.
