# "Money is no longer motivating": the software developer who stopped working at 34

> **In short:** Brandon Ganch, the *Mad Fientist*, saved about 70% of his after-tax income, first in rural Vermont and then in Edinburgh, and left his software job in July 2016 at 34. His story stands out for its honesty about the money and about the surprisingly hard emotional side of early retirement.

## The starting point

Brandon was, as one profile put it, an average guy with a software developer's job. He and his wife lived frugally in the woods of Vermont, and he started tracking every part of their finances in detail. He later shared the spreadsheet that helped him get there.

## The turning point

Once he understood how a high savings rate shortens the road to freedom, he went all in, saving around 70% of what he took home. Along the way he started the *Mad Fientist* blog and podcast.

## The plan

- **Save about 70% of after-tax income.**
- **Optimise every detail,** especially taxes and retirement accounts.
- **Move closer to family.** The couple moved to Scotland to be nearer his wife's family.

## The hard parts

This is the most valuable part of his story. On the first Monday without work, he panicked. It took him about three years to stop obsessing over money. He also admitted that his extreme frugality went too far: *"I got into deprivation and wasn't happy."* In year three came what he called his most complicated lesson: learning to just say no.

## Life after FI

He travels, spends time with friends and family, and works on hobbies. His biggest mindset shift, in his own words, was *"the realization that money is no longer motivating."* Once money stopped being the goal, he had to find new reasons to get out of bed, and he did, through projects he cares about.

## Lessons worth stealing

- **Try your post-FI life before you get there.** He recommends living as much of your "after" life as you can now, so you know what you're aiming for.
- **Don't confuse frugality with deprivation.** Cut what you don't value, not what makes you happy.
- **Plan for the emotional side.** Leaving work changes your identity, your routine and your social life, not just your bank balance.

## What it would look like in Denmark

His move to Scotland is a reminder that FIRE isn't only an American idea. It works in Europe, but the details change.

In Denmark, the "optimise every account" approach looks different. *Ratepension* and *aldersopsparing* give tax benefits but are locked until a few years before state pension age (currently 67 and rising). So an early retiree here typically needs two pots: freely available investments (an *aktiesparekonto*, then a normal investment account) to live on in the early years, and pension savings that take over later. Getting that split right matters as much as the savings rate itself.
