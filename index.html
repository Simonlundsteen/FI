Financial independence

Work because you want to, not because you have to.

Financial independence (FI) means your investments can cover your living costs indefinitely. Paid work becomes optional. You get there by spending less than you earn, investing the difference, and reaching one number: your FI number.

How FI works

Four ideas cover almost everything you need to know.

1

Know your spending

Your FI number depends on what you spend each year, not what you earn. Lower costs mean a lower target.

2

The 4% rule

Historically, a diversified portfolio has supported withdrawing about 4% of its starting value each year, adjusted for inflation, for 30+ years.

3

Savings rate is the lever

The share of income you save decides how fast you get there. It both grows your investments and shrinks the target.

4

Let compounding work

Invested money earns returns, and those returns earn returns. Starting early matters more than starting big.

How to calculate your FI number FI number = yearly expenses ÷ 0.04  =  yearly expenses × 25

For example, if you spend 300,000 a year you need about 7,500,000 invested. Flip it around and every amount you invest supports a yearly cost of 4% of that amount: 10,000 invested can pay for 400 a year, for good.

Keep in mind: the 4% rule comes from historical US market data. It's a planning rule of thumb, not a guarantee. Many people use 3–3.5% for early retirement or extra safety. Taxes on investment income (for example Danish aktieindkomst) and fees also matter. This site is educational and not financial advice.
